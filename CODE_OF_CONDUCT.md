# Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

We are committed to making participation in this project a respectful,
harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible
or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and
expression, level of experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality,
personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

We commit to acting and interacting in ways that contribute to an open,
welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Expected Behavior

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive community include:

- demonstrating empathy and respect toward other people;
- giving and accepting constructive technical feedback;
- focusing disagreement on observable behavior, evidence, and interoperability;
- acknowledging mistakes, correcting them, and learning from the experience;
- respecting privacy, confidentiality, and responsible disclosure boundaries;
- prioritizing the health of the project and community over individual
  preferences.

## Unacceptable Behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- sexualized language, imagery, attention, or advances;
- trolling, insults, derogatory comments, or personal or political attacks;
- public or private harassment, intimidation, or threats;
- publishing another person's private information without explicit permission;
- impersonation or deliberate misrepresentation of another contributor;
- knowingly submitting credentials, private data, malicious content, or
  undisclosed security exploits;
- sustained disruption of reviews, issues, discussions, or project operations;
- other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

Good-faith disagreement about specification design is not harassment. It must
remain relevant, evidence-based, and respectful.

## Scope

This code applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions,
commits, automation, and other project spaces. It also applies when someone
officially represents the project in public or private spaces.

Automated agents and their operators are subject to the same standards. The
human directing an agent is responsible for its project interactions.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to the repository maintainers using a private
contact method listed on their GitHub profiles. Do not disclose sensitive report
details in a public issue. If no safe maintainer contact is available, use
GitHub's abuse reporting tools.

Reports should include the relevant URLs, dates, participants, description, and
any supporting evidence. Maintainers will protect the reporter's privacy as far
as reasonably possible and will disclose information only as needed to evaluate
and address the report. Knowingly false or retaliatory reports violate this code.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards. They
may remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other
contributions that violate this code.

Responses will be proportionate to the behavior, context, impact, and history:

1. **Correction**: private explanation and request to correct the behavior.
2. **Warning**: formal warning with conditions for continued participation.
3. **Temporary restriction**: time-limited restriction from project interaction.
4. **Permanent restriction**: permanent removal from project participation.

Immediate restriction may be used for credible threats, severe harassment,
exposure of private information, malicious activity, or risks to participant
safety. Retaliation against a reporter or participant in an investigation is
prohibited.

Maintainers involved in a report must recuse themselves when reasonably
possible. Enforcement decisions and appeals should be reviewed by an uninvolved
maintainer. If none is available, the remaining maintainers will document and
manage the conflict as transparently as privacy permits.
